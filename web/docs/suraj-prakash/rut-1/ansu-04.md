# Rut 1, Ansu 4
## The yagya performed

*Dohra*

Having done all his father's rites as the elders directed, Sikhs and masaṅds came in a body from wherever they had heard of it.[^1] The Bedi lineage and the Trehans met there, and the Bhallas heard and came too. They sat close by the Guru, and stayed in shivirs of their own.[^2]

*Padhari Chhand*

Sri Mother Nanaki lamented. She shed tears, and her mind grew weak. Gujri too raised great grief, and daily the water ran from her eyes.[^3] Satguru considered it and sent the masaṅds. "Go and give the mothers great patience. There should be no grieving in the Guru's house. Let them abide in one unbroken anaṅd."[^4]

Hearing this they went to the mahal and saw them. They made namaskār and sat in their places. Joining their hands they said, "Mother, listen. So much attachment as this is not fitting for you.[^5] The Guru gave up the body freely. He kept the dharam of the world, and upheld the Hindus. The Turk lord took the sin of that head upon himself. The last hour of the body had come.[^6] Now look on the son in his place, and hold the mind's leaning back from grief."

Kirpal, the brother, was standing near, and hearing this talk he said at once,[^7] "Sister, take thought in your mind. What is to be never fails to be. Satguru wrote a shalok and sent it. Why do you not settle its meaning in your heart?[^8] Their son will be a Guru, and lofty; his glory is plain to see. Look on him and take anaṅd. He is dear to all as the moon is to the chakor.[^9] He said it again and again in what he sent you: hold affection, go and give them comfort."

Hearing it, both mothers considered well of it in their minds; they put grief aside and held love for the son.[^10] "As the custom is for the tying of the pag, the things needed should be brought in. If they can be found in this place, then buy them here; if not, send money to another city."[^11] Hearing it, the masaṅds sent and gave out money. They had a number of pags and cloths brought in. Costly wool and cloth were sent for, and then silk stuffs were fetched and stored.[^12] Money was given to a number of villages, and abundant ghee. White sugar was gathered and heaped up. Such things were all made ready. As much as was wanted was taken, and more.[^13]

The word was spoken and heard through the country. They come with longing in their hearts, every one of them. Servants and masaṅds made themselves ready. They took costly and beautiful dastārs.[^14] Gathering the saṅgat, they told out the greatness of it. "The Guru is of new years, and his abhishek is to be. There will be a great gathering at Anandpur. Join and come quickly; it is a lofty festival."[^15] In all four directions the Sikhs heard at once. They made no delay in setting out. On the road were great trains of the saṅgat. Meeting throng upon throng, they arrive quickly.[^16]

A great crowd goes on gathering at Anandpur. The Sikhs meet each other and talk. Hearing Satguru's qualities again and again, they are in anaṅd. His body of new years, his face like the moon.[^17] They all make namaskār and look on the form. His understanding quick and clever, beautiful, matchless. "Blessed, blessed is the Guru," their mouths say. His eyes are lively and modest and wide.[^18]

Then the mother said to all the masaṅds, "Perform a yagya, and get many kinds of things for it. Call the hosts of sādhs and saṅts from wherever they are, and give them food with honour."[^19] The Guru's officers heard a hukam of this kind and quickly set about the work in that way. They called for cooks. All were put to the work, each as was fitting.[^20]

Sweets were brought in, and much ghee was given. Wheat flour and maidā were taken. Many pūps and pūrikās were then cooked, and piled in a heap in a good place.[^21] Many great karāhīs were set on the fire. They prepare the tihāval and go on setting it down. Many bare and kachaurīs were made ready, and skilled hands set to it quickly.[^22] The cooking was accomplished in many ways, and heaps of pūp and pūrikā were piled. Wherever there were mahaṅts and saṅts of holy garb, from there they called them and brought them.[^23]

Companies come in crowds from here and there. Many Brahmins came, and people of other jātis. All were seated with fitting honour, and the Nāth climbed to a high mahal and looked on.[^24] The masaṅds make up the paṅgat. Wherever it seemed fitting, there they seat them. The crowd grew great, and the host gave them patience, speaking to them and settling them in a long paṅgat.[^25] The hosts began to serve out the food. They put down as much at once as would satisfy. Quickly they set the whole of it before everyone. Then all began to eat and to taste.[^26]

They praise it again and again. Their mouths say, "Why should such food not be? It is made for the sake of Gurdev, for the Guru's son, who is himself skilled in cause and effect."[^27] One distributed panchāmrit; another gave out pūp and pūrikā. They run and serve dahī bare with jīrā. Without being asked they watch, and pour out more.[^28] They are satisfied, and eat much that is savoury. "Take, take," they call, going about quickly. Much that is sweet, much that is sour and much that is pungent: all of it they eat, slowly, slowly.[^29] One gives clear water again and again. The mind delights, satisfied, tasting the food. Then, having drunk the water, they wash their hands. "Blessed, blessed is the Guru" is the word.[^30]

Then Satguru the wise, with the mother, gave much money into the masaṅds' hands. "Go and give to everyone, so that there is no uproar. Take it in order; let no one be left empty."[^31] The masaṅds took it and gave it to all. They marked out the dwijas, the saṅts and the mahaṅts especially. The whole assembly rejoiced and praised it greatly. "We have got glory in the Guru's first yagya.[^32] The Guru is complete in all kalā, and lofty. He is the like of Sri Hari Gobind. His zeal and his understanding are of the same nature, and his heart is very generous; so it is known.[^33] He makes the host of people lofty and blessed, and grants anaṅd, becoming merciful."

Hearing it another speaks, telling out a wonder. "What his father and his grandfather were![^34] Sri Arjan, and Guru Ram Das, who set free countless of their own servants. His birth is in such a line as that. Great is the fortune of those who stay near them."[^35] Telling such glory, all were satisfied, and dakshiṇā, money, was given. They give many blessings, taking anaṅd. "May the Guru's son's years be greatly increased.[^36] As long as Jamuna and Ganga are in the world, so long may you live in joy."

So the yagya was done, with a great crowd of people, and in the city of Anand there was great anaṅd.[^37] The Sikh saṅgat were all fully satisfied. Then as many masaṅds as there were ate the food. Men of many countries came and came, and pitched their shivirs with longing in their hearts.[^38] That day passed, and then the night passed. All sang the Guru's shabad at daybreak. There was a great festive clamour, and the minds of all delighted. Men meet each other and hold love.[^39]

"The auspicious month of Phalgun (February to March), the first paksha, the fifth day, counting the chief lagan: this was worked out for the tying of the dastār. Those of great understanding fixed it exactly."[^40] Word was sent out first and made known wherever it could be, and those who learned of it came quickly. Lavpuri and Sudhasar and the rest gathered; the Sikh saṅgat heard, and all took the road.[^41]

Those close to Sri Nanak, Angad and Amar, in whose houses was the rank of sitting on a mañjī, who had received the greatness of the blessing from the nine Gurus: all of them came into the city of Anand.[^42] The Sikh saṅgats pitched their shivirs in numbers, and made great encampments here and there. In all four directions, in the cities and villages nearby, the whole gathering heard of it and was in lofty anaṅd.[^43]

*Dohra*

They come and offer their gifts, bringing shardhā with them. "Blessed is the Guru, who kept the dastār on the head of the Hindu."[^44]

---
*Thus in the Sri Gur Pratap Suraj Granth, in the first Rut, the fourth ansu, named the telling of the prasang of "the yagya".*

*Sri Gur Pratap Suraj Granth, Bhai Santokh Singh, 1843, with the annotation of Bhai Vir Singh, 1927 to 1935 (BVS in the project docs). Cite as Rut 1.4.12.*

### Notes

[^1]: The father is Guru Tegh Bahadur Ji, and the rites are the cremation told in the ansu before this one. · masaṅd: an agent appointed by the Guru to lead a local saṅgat, gather its offerings and bring them in.
[^2]: The Bedis are the family of Guru Nanak Dev Ji, the Trehans of Guru Angad Dev Ji, the Bhallas of Guru Amar Das Ji. The Sodhis, the fourth of these families, are the Guru's own. · shivir: a camp, a pitched tent.
[^3]: Sri Mother Nanaki is Mata Nanaki, widow of Guru Hargobind Sahib Ji and mother of Guru Tegh Bahadur Ji. Gujri is Mata Gujri, Guru Tegh Bahadur Ji's wife and the mother of the boy Guru, who is nine years old.
[^4]: anaṅd: bliss.
[^5]: mahal: the house, here the mothers' quarters. · namaskār: the bow of greeting.
[^6]: dharam: righteous duty, the order that holds a world together. · The Turk lord is Aurangzeb, on whose order Guru Tegh Bahadur Ji was executed at Delhi.
[^7]: Kirpal is Kirpal Chand, Mata Gujri's brother and so the boy Guru's maternal uncle.
[^8]: shalok: a couplet. Guru Tegh Bahadur Ji's shaloks, sent from Delhi before his execution, stand at the very end of the Guru Granth Sahib.
[^9]: chakor: the bird said to gaze at the moon and to live on its light.
