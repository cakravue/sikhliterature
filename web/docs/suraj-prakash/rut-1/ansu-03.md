# Rut 1, Ansu 3
## The cremation of the head at Anandpur

*Dohra*

Sri Nanaki was there, and Gujri with her, and Satguru went to them. "Where shall we cremate the Guru's head?" He asked how it might best be arranged.[^1]

*Svaiya*

The whole body of masaṅds spoke with folded hands. "Guru Hargobind Ji was cremated at Kiratpur, and from that day the whole line of the Sodhis has been cremated within that city. Now let it be as you wish; weigh it with sound judgement."

Sri Gujri heard them out and did not agree, and she spoke aloud.[^2] "The others had founded no city of their own, and for that reason they carried their dead there and cremated them. He who took up the Guruship at that time left his former home and rose with this in his heart: that the kinsmen keep their envy, and that it is not good to fall into quarrels. Ever of peaceful and matchless form, he came to this place and saw that it was auspicious.[^3] The masaṅds gave money, land was bought, and every kind of effort was made. Seeing how great the place was, he gave it the name Anand, and by that name it became known. Bring the head here and do the cremation here. His aged mother weeps bitterly. Let her look on that beautiful face; know that this is best of all."[^4]

As she said it her throat filled, and out of great love the words would not come. The perfect Guru heard his mother out, knew that she was right, and it pleased his mind. He made ready to bring the head into the city, and settled both mothers in the house. He took a great company with him, and the whole gathering lifted up the head and set out.[^5] A heap of money was piled up, and the beautiful bimān was raised and carried at the head of them all. The masaṅds, and the Sikhs and servants who had come, followed close behind Sri Guru. They recited the great Gurbānī as they went, water running from their eyes. They came into the city of Kirat, and the whole gathering told out the Guru's glory.[^6] They went out beyond the town, to where the Sikh who had taken up the Guru's head was. Men and women heard the whole matter with their own ears, and there was terrible anguish and grief. Crying out, stricken with pain, they all went and joined him, taking the sorrow upon themselves. At once the head was laid within the bimān, and it was lifted, and the whole gathering bowed.[^7]

Chaur whisks were waved from all four sides, and costly dushālās were laid on in numbers. One sprinkled rose water, another a wonderful gulāl; great woven garlands were placed upon it. Perfumes were poured out in heaps, and such a fragrance rose as they went forward. They sang Sri Guru's words in their rāgs, with a host of mridaṅg and rabāb together.[^8] They sang Maru joined with Vadhans, standing in front and setting a pauṛī to it. They took money along the road of the city, and then scattered it over the bimān. They strewed golden flowers and lāj, and men and women grieved to see it. Some of the servants took the coin by handfuls and rained it down.[^9] Slowly, slowly they came on toward Anandpur, raising a loud lament. The masaṅds, the servants and the Sikhs went on all four sides of the bimān. Conches were sounded with force. They cursed the Turk emperor and spoke abuse of him. "May his root fall into the salt sea and burn away. He has proved an unworthy son; the rule of his line will be destroyed."[^10]

When they came near Anandpur, child and elder, men and women, heard of it. Sri Gujri left the house and came out of the city to meet them on the way. Mother Nanaki was kept back, strengthless and aged, bearing a heavy affliction. All the rest came out in haste, and there was a wailing beyond what was fitting.[^11] Seeing the great splendour of the bimān, she remembered in her heart her husband's great qualities. "Your son's years are not many; his aged mother bears great affliction." She made her baṅdan with folded hands, bowed her head and shed tears. Then they brought the bimān to that place where the load of sandalwood had been set out ready.[^12] Mother Nanaki came and met it there, calling out and speaking his name aloud. "My son, of peaceful and matchless form, let me look on that face this once. I shall die of grief now. How is there any living, in this manner of suffering?" As soon as this lament was heard, the Guru's leave was taken and the bimān was set down.[^13]

They drew back all the cloths and looked on Sri Guru's face, Gujri and her son together. Mother Nanaki looks on, weeping hard, laying her own hands upon him. As though the light were living still, so auspiciously did the face and forehead shine. Both eyes are closed in such a way as the petals of a lotus shrink when the water is gone.[^14] Black and white hair mingled, shining on the beard and on the face. The masaṅds, the great Sikhs and the servants long to look. A crowd had gathered; all bow, holding great love, their hearts shaken. They made the covering and built the pyre, and grief and sorrow pricked them like a thorn.[^15]

They laid him on the sandalwood pyre, and every mind was humbled in that hour. The servants gave the fire into Sri Guru's hand, and it was kindled, and the flame was bright on all four sides. Then they made pardachhan and bowed in salutation, holding great love. Kalgīdhar performed the whole rite, and the throng of people bathed in the water.[^16] They all sat down on the bank of the Satluj, singing the Guru's comforting word. With one mind, one listens with his ears; another holds dhiān on the Guru's form, dear to the mind. They gave tilāñjul for Sri Pati, as the custom has always run in the world. They took the wet clothes off the body, put on others, and settled there.[^17]

*Dohra*

Hearing it, they sang the shabad, and then set a pauṛī to it. They took the bhog, did ardās, and every one of them bowed his head.[^18]

*Svaiya*

The tihāval came in plenty, and was distributed among the whole saṅgat. Putting Sri Kalgīdhar in front, they went slowly to their homes. All went and settled in the sitting place, and gave Sri Guru long praise. "He has become the very limit of patience in the world, and holds forgiveness; it is not to be found in anyone else.[^19] The lord of Delhi has committed a great crime; being of foolish mind, he will get the pain of hell. He kept the support of the Hindu by giving his head, so that the maryādā that had come down from before might go on. Otherwise the Vedas, the Puranas, the tīraths, the devtās, the dehurās, the whole way of it would have been wiped out. Who is there but Sri Gurdev who would give his life and set dharam firm in the world?[^20] Blessed for ever is Guru Tegh Bahadur, who did the deed of courage and showed that it could be kept."

Then the Sikh who had brought the head from the city was questioned, and they seated him near. "How did you come by it? Did no one see you? Were you nowhere stopped? They had many men standing on watch; how did they lose their wits? Tell us."[^21]

He joined his hands and gave his account. "The work was done by Gurdev's help. The moment the head flew from the body, a great dust rose and fell in everyone's eyes. Nobody looked; I took it up, and my heart did not fear. An uproar broke out among them all; hearing it I moved and went quickly, and no one laid hold of me.[^22] Many men were standing at the gateway. I took courage and ran at speed. Not one of them stopped me or challenged me; I left the city and went my way. Afterward they all set about searching, and were astonished when they did not find it. They guessed that someone had carried it off, and mounted a body of riders.[^23]

"They came up with me some way off, and when I saw them my mind was afraid. Seeing carts going along the road, I set the Guru's head in one of them. Those riders were shouting threats, looking at and searching whatever man they came upon. They came to me and said, 'Stand still', and had my clothes opened, and looked.[^24] Then they set about inspecting the carts, stopping them and beginning the search. When they looked at the one behind, the head had gone to the one in front. When it was not found there, they turned eagerly to search the ones in front, and the head came back again into the one behind. The fools were beaten; so were they cheated.[^25]

*Dohra*

"The Guru's head was visible to me alone; no other could see it. The men gave up searching the road and went back to their own city.[^26] When they had gone out of sight and were left behind, I took the head up, wrapped it in cloths, and carried it on my own head.[^27] I did not tire on the road, nor did fear come on me in the night. I went fearless and of one mind, as the hukam had been.[^28]

"As the Guru had explained it to me, so I did, without a doubt. He became my helper himself; he gave me a single jām.[^29] He brought me out from there and delivered me here, though many stood on guard. The tilak and the janeū of the Hindu: Satguru kept their support.[^30] An uproar has fallen on Delhi, and I know nothing of it. Ask that closely of whoever comes from there; he will tell it."[^31]

---
*Thus in the Sri Gur Pratap Suraj Granth, in the first Rut, the third ansu, named the telling of the prasang of "the cremation of the head".*

*Sri Gur Pratap Suraj Granth, Bhai Santokh Singh, 1843, with the annotation of Bhai Vir Singh (BVS in the notes), 1927 to 1935. Cite as Rut 1.3.12.*

### Notes

[^1]: Satguru here is Guru Gobind Rai, nine years old, Guru since his father's death. Sri Nanaki is Guru Tegh Bahadur Ji's mother; Gujri is his wife and the boy's mother. The head is Guru Tegh Bahadur Ji's, brought from Delhi in the ansu before this one.
[^2]: masaṅd: an agent appointed by the Guru to lead a local saṅgat and bring in its offerings. · The Sodhis are the Guru's own family. · Kiratpur: the town on the Satluj founded by Guru Hargobind Sahib Ji, where the family's dead had been cremated since his time.
[^3]: The one who took up the Guruship is Guru Tegh Bahadur Ji.
[^4]: The aged mother is Mata Nanaki.
[^5]:
[^6]: bimān: the bier on which a body is carried in procession. · Gurbānī: the revealed word of the Gurus. · The city of Kirat is Kiratpur.

[^8]: chaur: the whisk waved over what is honoured. · dushālā: a costly shawl of two widths. · gulāl: coloured powder. · mridaṅg: a two-headed drum. · rabāb: a stringed instrument.
[^9]: Maru and Vadhans: two rāgs, sung at a death. · pauṛī: a stanza of a var, sung. · lāj: parched grain, scattered as an offering.
[^10]: The Turk emperor is Aurangzeb. · BVS: "Its root has fallen into the salt sea. That is, it will burn away and not remain."

[^18]: shabad: a hymn of the Gurus, the divine word. · bhog: the completing of a reading. · ardās: the standing petition made at the end of it.
[^19]: tihāval: karah prashad, made of flour, ghee and sugar in equal parts. · saṅgat: the gathered congregation.
