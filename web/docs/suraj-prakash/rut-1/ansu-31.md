# Rut 1, Ansu 31

## Bhim Chand's messenger comes to ask for the elephant

*Dohra*

ਇਸ ਪ੍ਰਕਾਰ ਸ਼੍ਰੀ ਸਤਿਗੁਰੂ ਕਰਤਿ ਅਨੇਕ ਬਿਲਾਸ।\
ਕਰੀ ਪ੍ਰਸਾਦੀ ਪਰ ਚਢੈਂ ਮਹਿਦ ਸ਼ਿੰਗਾਰੈਂ ਤਾਸ ॥੧॥

In this way the Satguru took his pleasure in many things. He would ride out on the elephant Prasadi,[^1] and have it richly adorned. (1)

*Chaupai*

ਬਨ ਮਹਿਂ ਬਿਚਰਹਿਂ ਪੁਨ ਪੁਰਿ ਆਵਹਿਂ। ਅਨਿਕ ਅਖੇਰ ਘਾਤ ਕਰਿ ਲਯਾਵਹਿਂ।\
ਜਿਨ ਜੀਵਨ ਕੇ ਪੁੰਨ ਪ੍ਰਕਾਸ਼ੇ। ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਹਾਥ ਬਨਹਿ ਤਿਨ ਨਾਸ਼ੇ ॥੨॥

He would roam in the forest and then come back to the town, bringing in game of many kinds that he had killed. Those creatures whose merit had come to light met their end at the Satguru's hand. (2)

ਤੇਜ ਤੁਰੰਗਮ ਪਰ ਆਰੋਹੈਂ। ਚਲਤਿ ਫੰਦਾਵਤਿ ਮ੍ਰਿਗ ਜਿਮ ਸੋਹੈ।\
ਬਹੁ ਸ਼ਸਤ੍ਰਨਿ ਅੱਭਯਾਸ ਕਮਾਵਹਿਂ। ਨਰ ਢਿਗਵਰਤੀ ਕੋ ਸਿਖਰਾਵਹਿਂ ॥੩॥

He would mount a swift horse and make it leap like a deer as it went, and he looked splendid. He practised much with his weapons, and taught the men about him. (3)

ਤੋਮਰ, ਤੀਰ, ਤੁਪਕ, ਤਰਵਾਰ। ਤਬਰ, ਤਮਾਂਚੇ, ਤੇਗ਼ੇ ਚਾਰੁ।\
ਬਖਸ਼ਹਿਂ ਸੁਭਟਨਿ ਕੋ ਹਰਖਾਵਹਿਂ। ਆਪ ਚਲਾਇ ਤਿਨਹੁਂ ਦਿਖਰਾਵਹਿਂ ॥੪॥

Lances, arrows, muskets and swords, battle-axes, pistols and fine broadswords: he gave them to his warriors and delighted them, and he wielded them himself and showed them how. (4)

ਅਧਿਕ ਸੂਰਤਾ ਕਰਹਿਂ ਸਰਾਹਿਨਿ। 'ਲਰਕਤਿ ਹੋਇਂ ਲੋਕ ਭਟ ਬਾਹਨ।\
ਬਿਜੈ ਕਰਹਿਂ ਭੂਤਲ ਸੁਖ ਭੋਗਹਿਂ। ਰਨ ਕਰਿ ਮਰਹਿਂ ਜਾਤਿ ਸੁਰ ਲੋਗਹਿਂ ॥੫॥

He praised courage highly. "Both worlds hang on a warrior's arms.[^2] If he wins, he enjoys ease on the earth; if he dies in battle, he goes to the world of the gods. (5)

ਤਹਾਂ ਅਨੇਕ ਅਨੰਦ ਮਿਲੈ ਹੈਂ। ਸੁਜਸੁ ਬਿਲੰਦ ਸਭਿਨਿ ਮਹਿਂ ਪੈ ਹੈ।'\
ਦੇਸ਼ ਬਿਦੇਸ਼ਨ ਸੰਗਤਿ ਆਵਹਿ। ਦਰਸਹਿ, ਮਨੋ ਕਾਮਨਾ ਪਾਵਹਿ ॥੬॥

"There he will find joys of many kinds, and high fame among all." The saṅgat came from their own lands and from far countries, saw him, and gained what their hearts desired. (6)

ਸਿੱਖਨਿ ਕੋ ਸ਼ੁਭ ਮਤਿ ਨਿਤ ਦੇਤਿ। ਭਗਤਿ ਗਯਾਨ ਵੈਰਾਗ ਸਮੇਤ।\
ਇਸ ਪ੍ਰਕਾਰ ਨਿਤ ਸਮਾ ਬਿਤਾਵੈਂ। ਭਏ ਤਰੁਨ ਗੁਰ ਰੂਪ ਸੁਹਾਵੈ ॥੭॥

Every day he gave the Sikhs good counsel, with devotion, knowledge and vairāg.[^3] So the days went by, and the Guru grew into a young man, beautiful in form. (7)

ਕੇਤਿਕ ਸਮਾ ਬਿਤੀਤਨਿ ਭਯੋ। ਜਬਿ ਕੋ ਭੀਮਚੰਦ ਮਿਲਿ ਗਯੋ।\
ਮਨ ਮੂਰਖ ਕੇ ਚਿਤਵਨਿ ਸੋਈ। ਲੈਬੇ ਦੁਰਦ ਪ੍ਰਸਾਦੀ ਜੋਈ ॥੮॥

Some time had passed since Bhim Chand[^4] had met him, and the fool kept turning one thing over in his mind: how to take the elephant Prasadi. (8)

ਅਨਿਕ ਜਤਨ ਕੋ ਚਿਤਵਤਿ ਰਹੈ। -ਕਿਸ ਪ੍ਰਕਾਰ ਕਰਿ ਗੁਰ ਤੇ ਲਹੈਂ।\
ਪ੍ਰਥਮ ਲਰਨਿ ਕੀ ਬਾਤ ਨ ਆਛੇ। ਲੇਉਂ ਕਪਟ ਕਰਿ -ਨਿਤ ਮਨ ਬਾਂਛੇ ॥੯॥

He kept thinking of one scheme after another. *How am I to get it from the Guru? To begin with fighting would not be good. I shall take it by a trick.* So he wished, day after day. (9)

ਇਕ ਦਿਨ ਬੈਠਿ ਸਭਾ ਕੇ ਮਾਂਹੀ। ਸ਼ੁਭ ਮਤਿ ਕਰੇ ਹਕਾਰਨਿ ਪਾਹੀ।\
ਜਿਤਿਕ ਮੁਸਾਹਿਬ ਅਪਰ ਵਜ਼ੀਰ। ਆਇ ਥਿਰੇ ਭੂਪਤਿ ਕੇ ਤੀਰ ॥੧੦॥

One day he sat in his court and summoned his wise men. All his courtiers and the other wazirs came and stood beside the raja. (10)

ਸਭਿ ਢਿਗ ਭਨਯੋ ਮਨੋਰਥ ਆਪਨ। ਜਿਸ ਪ੍ਰਕਾਰ ਚਿਤਵਤਿ ਚਿਤ ਆਪਨ।\
'ਮੈਂ ਚਾਹਿਤਿ ਗੁਰ ਤੇ ਗਜ ਲੀਨੋ। ਅਚਰਜ ਮਹਾਂ ਬਿਲੋਕਨਿ ਕੀਨੋ ॥੧੧॥

In front of them all he told them his wish, just as he had been turning it over in his heart. "I want to take the elephant from the Guru. I have seen a great wonder. (11)

ਕਿਸ ਤੇ ਆਗੇ ਸੁਨਯੋ ਨ ਐਸੇ। ਪਿਖਿਨਿ ਬਿਲੋਚਨ ਤੌ ਹੁਇ ਕੈਸੇ।\
ਲਾਇਕ ਪਤਿਸ਼ਾਹਨ ਕੇ ਪਾਸੀ। ਪਰਚਤਿ ਚਿਤ ਜਿਸ ਪਿਖਤਿ ਹੁਲਾਸੀ ॥੧੨॥

"I have never heard of such a thing from anyone before; how would the eyes ever get to see one? It is fit to stand beside Patshahs. The heart is held and gladdened at the sight of it. (12)

ਕੈ ਹਮ ਤੇ ਮਹਿਪਾਲਕ ਭਾਰੇ। ਸ਼ੋਭਤ ਦੀਰਘ, ਥਿਰ ਹ੍ਵੈ ਦ੍ਵਾਰੇ।\
ਜਯੋਂ ਕਯੋਂ ਕਰਿ ਲੇਨੋ ਬਨਿ ਆਵੈ। ਕਰਹਿਂ ਕਪਟ ਕਾਰਜ ਸਰ ਜਾਵੈ ॥੧੩॥

"Or else at the gate of rajas greater than we are; standing there, it looks splendid in its size. I must have it, by whatever way it can be got. Let us work a trick and get the business done. (13)

ਹੋਇ ਸਰਲ ਜੇ ਜਾਚਹਿਂ ਗੁਰ ਤੇ। ਨਹਿਂ ਦੇ ਸਕਹਿਂ ਪ੍ਰੇਮ ਬਹੁ ਉਰਤੇ।\
ਰਚਹੁ ਘਾਤ ਐਸੀ ਜਿਮ ਦੇਹਿਂ। ਏਕਬਾਰ ਕਯੋਂਹੂੰ ਕਰ ਲੇਹਿਂ' ॥੧੪॥

"If we ask the Guru plainly, he will not be able to give it; he loves it dearly. Lay such a snare that he gives it. Let us once get it into our hands, somehow." (14)

ਭੀਮਚੰਦ ਕੋ ਸੁਨਿ ਕਰਿ ਆਸ਼ੈ। ਕਰਿ ਬਿਚਾਰ ਪੁਨ ਬਚਨ ਪ੍ਰਕਾਸ਼ੈ।\
'ਇਹ ਤੋ ਨਹੀਂ ਕਠਨ ਕੁਛ ਬਾਤ। ਗਜ ਲੈਬੇ ਕੀ ਪ੍ਰਾਪਤਿ ਘਾਤ ॥੧੫॥

When they had heard Bhim Chand's purpose, they considered it and then spoke. "This is no hard matter at all. A way to get the elephant is ready to hand. (15)

ਜੋ ਰਾਵਰ ਕੋ ਨੰਦਨ ਟੀਕਾ। ਤਿਸ ਉਮੈਦ ਹੈ ਆਵਨ ਟੀਕਾ।\
ਫਤੇਸ਼ਾਹ ਭੇਜਯੋ ਅਬਿ ਚਾਹਤਿ। ਸੁਤਾ ਬਯਾਹ ਕੋ ਕਰਨਿ ਉਮਾਹਤਿ ॥੧੬॥

"Your son is your heir,[^5] and there is hope of a betrothal mark coming for him. Fateh Shah[^6] wants to send it now; he is eager to marry his daughter. (16)

ਹਿਤ ਬਰਾਤ ਮੈਂ ਲੇ ਕਰਿ ਜਾਨੇ। ਇਮ ਜਾਚੇ ਤੇ ਨਹਿਂ ਗੁਰ ਮਾਨੇ।\
ਜਾਨੈ ਬਹੁਤ ਦਿਵਸ ਮੈਂ ਐਹੈ। ਕਰਹਿਂ ਫਰੇਬ ਨ ਕੁੰਚਰ ਦੈਹੈਂ ॥੧੭॥

"Ask for the elephant to take in the wedding procession, and the Guru will not agree to it. He will know that it will be many days before it comes back, so he will make excuses and not give the elephant. (17)

ਯਾਂ ਤੇ ਇਸ ਪ੍ਰਕਾਰ ਨਰ ਜਾਇ। ਸਹਿਤ ਬੇਨਤੀ ਬਾਕ ਅਲਾਇ।\
-ਸਿਰੀ ਨਗਰ ਤੇ ਸਾਕ ਹਮਾਰੇ। ਲਾਗੀ ਪਠੇ ਆਇ ਇਸ ਬਾਰੇ ॥੧੮॥

"So let a man go and speak this way, with a petition. 'Our kin by marriage in Srinagar have sent their go-betweens[^7] here on this business. (18)

ਤਿਨਹੁਂ ਦਿਖਾਵਨਿ ਕੇ ਹਿਤ ਚਹੀਅਹਿ। ਵਸਤੁ ਅਜਾਇਬ ਤੁਮ ਢਿਗ ਲਹੀਅਹਿ।\
ਕਰਹੁ ਕ੍ਰਿਪਾ ਜਾਚਯੋ ਗਜ ਦੀਜੈ। ਰਾਜਨ ਕੋ ਘਰ ਅਪਨਿ ਜਨੀਜੈ ॥੧੯॥

"'To show them, we need the wondrous thing that is to be found with you. Be gracious and give the elephant we ask for; count the raja's house as your own. (19)

ਨਰ ਸਾਕਨਿ ਕੇ ਤਿਨਹੁਂ ਦਿਖਾਇ। ਪੁਨ ਹਾਥੀ ਇਹ ਦੇਹਿਂ ਪੁਚਾਇ-।'\
ਜਬਹਿ ਮੁਸਾਹਿਬ ਮੰਤ੍ਰ ਬਤਾਇਵ। ਭੀਮਚੰਦ ਸੁਨਿ ਕੈ ਹਰਖਾਇਵ ॥੨੦॥

"'Once it has been shown to the men of our kin by marriage, we shall have the elephant sent back to you.'" When the courtiers had set out this counsel, Bhim Chand heard it and was glad. (20)

ਕਰੀ ਸਰਾਹਨਿ ਬੁੱਧਿ ਬਡੇਰੀ। 'ਇਸ ਬਿਧਿ ਹੀ ਆਵਤਿ ਮਤਿ ਮੇਰੀ।\
ਏਕ ਬਾਰ ਕੁੰਚਰ ਸੋ ਪਾਊਂ। ਬਹੁਰ ਨ ਦੇਉਂ ਨਿਕਟਿ ਰਖਵਾਊਂ' ॥੨੧॥

He praised their wisdom highly. "This is just what I had in mind myself. Once I get that elephant, I shall not give it back; I shall keep it by me." (21)

ਸਭਿਹੂੰ ਮਸਲਤ ਇਹ ਠਹਿਰਾਈ। -ਗਜ ਪ੍ਰਾਪਤਿ ਹ੍ਵੈ- ਇਮ ਮਨ ਭਾਈ।\
ਤਬਿ ਵਕੀਲ ਕੋ ਨਿਕਟਿ ਹਕਾਰਾ। ਸਗਲ ਭੇਦ ਤਿਸ ਪਾਸ ਉਚਾਰਾ ॥੨੨॥

They all settled on this counsel; *let the elephant be got* was what pleased their minds. Then Bhim Chand called his envoy[^8] to him and told him the whole of the secret. (22)

'ਗੁਰ ਢਿਗ ਜਾਹੁ ਪ੍ਰਸਾਦੀ ਲਯਾਵਹੁ। ਅਪਨੀ ਬੁਧਿ ਬਿਸਾਲ ਉਪਜਾਵਹੁ।\
ਬਿਨਤੀ ਸਹਿਤ ਰਚਹੁ ਚਤੁਰਾਈ। ਜਿਸ ਤੇ ਨਿਜ ਕਾਰਜ ਬਨਿ ਆਈ ॥੨੩॥

"Go to the Guru and bring Prasadi back. Bring all your wit to bear. Make your petition, and work your cleverness with it, so that our business gets done. (23)

ਕਹਹੁ ਕੂਰ ਬਡ ਕਪਟ ਸਮੇਤਾ। ਜਯੋਂ ਕਯੋਂ ਆਨੋ ਕਰੀ ਨਿਕੇਤਾ।\
ਜੇ ਕਰਿ ਇਹ ਕਾਰਜ ਕਰਿ ਲਯਾਵਹਿਂ। ਬਹੁ ਬਖਸ਼ੀਸ ਮੋਹਿ ਤੇ ਪਾਵਹਿਂ ॥੨੪॥

"Tell lies, with great deceit, and bring the elephant home somehow. If you carry this off and bring it back, you will have large rewards from me. (24)

ਜਾਨੀ ਜਾਇ ਸੁਮਤਿ ਜਿਮ ਤੇਰੀ। ਸਕਲ ਪ੍ਰਕਾਰ ਖੁਸ਼ੀ ਹੁਇ ਮੇਰੀ।\
ਜਿਮ ਦੇਖਹਿਂ ਗੁਰ ਕੋ ਰੁਖ ਮਹਾਂ। ਹੁਇ ਅਨੁਸਾਰੀ ਬੋਲਹੁ ਤਹਾਂ' ॥੨੫॥

"We shall see how good your wits are, and I shall be pleased with you in every way. Watch the Guru's mood closely, and speak in keeping with it." (25)

ਇੱਤਯਾਦਿਕ ਬਨਾਇ ਬਹੁ ਬਾਤੀ। ਸਿਖਰਾਵਤਿ ਰਚਿਬੇ ਹਿਤ ਘਾਤੀ।\
ਮਹਿਮਾ ਲਖਹਿ ਨ -ਇਹ ਜਗ ਸ੍ਵਾਮੀ। ਸਕਲ ਘਟਨਿ ਕੋ ਅੰਤਰਿ ਜਾਮੀ ॥੨੬॥

He taught the envoy many such things to make his snare. Bhim Chand did not see the greatness of the one he meant to trap: that this was the lord of the world, who knows what is within every heart. (26)

ਅਤਿ ਮਤਿ ਕੇ ਰਤਨਾਕਰ ਪੂਰੇ। ਬਡ ਗੰਭੀਰ ਚਰਿਤ ਜਿਨ ਰੂਰੇ-।\
ਰੰਚਕ ਭਰ ਚਾਤੁਰਤਾ ਪਾਇ। ਛਲਯੋ ਚਹੈ ਤਿਨ ਲਖਿ ਅਲਪਾਇ ॥੨੭॥

He is an ocean brimming with wisdom, deep beyond measure, and his deeds are beautiful. With a speck of cleverness to his name, a man of little understanding wanted to deceive him. (27)

ਮਾਰਤੰਡ ਮੰਡਲ ਬਡ ਚੰਡੰ। ਜੋ ਦੀਪਤਿ ਬ੍ਰਹਮੰਡ ਅਖੰਡੰ।\
ਲਖਹਿਂ ਸੁਰਾਸੁਰ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਸਾਚਾ। ਛਲਯੋ ਚਹਤਿ ਤਿਨ ਕੋ ਮਤਿ ਕਾਚਾ ॥੨੮॥

He is the great blazing orb of the sun, whose light shines through the whole unbroken universe, and gods and demons alike know him as the true Satguru; and the man of unripe mind wanted to deceive him. (28)

ਗਰਬ ਕਰਹਿ ਉਰ ਜਯੋਂ ਖੱਦਯੋਤ। ਉਡਿ ਉਡਿ ਨਿਜ ਦਿਖਰਾਇ ਉਦੋਤਿ।\
ਲੇ ਸਿੱਖਯਾ ਕੋ ਸਕਲ ਪ੍ਰਕਾਰੀ। ਚਲਿਬੇ ਹਿਤ ਵਕੀਲ ਭਾ ਤਯਾਰੀ ॥੨੯॥

He was like a firefly that takes pride in its heart, flying up and up to show off its own glow. The envoy took his instructions in every particular and made ready to go. (29)

ਨਿਜ ਰਾਜੇ ਕੋ ਧੀਰਜ ਦੀਨਿ। 'ਕਰਹੁਂ ਫਰੇਬ ਅਨੇਕ ਪ੍ਰਬੀਨ।\
ਆਨਯੋ ਹੀ ਜਾਨਹੁਂ ਮਨ ਮਾਂਹੀ। ਤੁਮ ਇਕਬਾਲ ਬਿਦਤਿ ਜਹਿਂ ਕਾਂਹੀ ॥੩੦॥

He reassured his raja. "I shall work many tricks, and cleverly. Count it in your mind as brought already. Your fortune is known everywhere. (30)

ਸੈਲੇਂਦ੍ਰਨਿ ਬ੍ਰਿੰਦਨ ਕੋ ਟੀਕਾ। ਜਥਾ ਗਜਿੰਦ੍ਰ ਮ੍ਰਿਗਨਿ ਮਹੁਂ ਨੀਕਾ।\
ਸਫਲਹਿਂ ਤੋਹਿ ਮਨੋਰਥ ਸਾਰੇ।' ਇਮ ਤਰੀਫ ਕਰਿ ਚਹਤਿ ਪਧਾਰੇ ॥੩੧॥

"You are the crown of all the lords of the hills, as the lord of elephants[^9] is finest among beasts. May all your wishes be fulfilled." With this flattery he asked leave to go. (31)

ਨਮੋ ਕਰਤਿ ਹਯ ਤਬਹਿ ਅਰੂਢਾ। ਗਮਨਯੋ ਪੰਥ ਚਿਤਹਿ ਛਲ ਗੂਢਾ।\
ਪਹੁਂਚਯੋ ਆਨਿ ਅਨੰਦ ਪੁਰਿ ਥਾਨਾ। ਕੀਨਸਿ ਡੇਰਾ ਹੇਰਿ ਮਕਾਨਾ ॥੩੨॥

He bowed, mounted his horse, and took the road, with the trick hidden deep in his heart. He reached Anandpur, looked about for lodging, and set up camp there. (32)

ਧਰਿ ਕਰਿ ਚੀਰ ਸਰੀਰ ਅਛੇਰੇ। ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਪੌਰ ਪਹੁਂਚਿ ਤਿਸ ਬੇਰੇ।\
ਦ੍ਵਾਰਪਾਲ ਕੋ ਭਨਤਿ ਜਨਾਈ। 'ਪਠਯੋ ਮੋਹਿ ਗੁਰ ਢਿਗ ਗਿਰਰਾਈ ॥੩੩॥

He put on fine clothes and went at once to the Satguru's gate, and made himself known to the doorkeeper. "The raja of the hills has sent me to the Guru. (33)

ਅੰਤਰ ਜਾਇ ਕਰਹੁ ਸੁਧਿ ਮੋਰੀ। ਕਰਹੁ ਬੰਦਨਾ ਜੁਗ ਕਰ ਜੋਰੀ।'\
ਸੁਨਤਿ ਪੌਰ ਪਤਿ ਤਿਸ ਠਹਿਰਾਯੋ। ਆਪ ਗਯੋ ਜਹਿਂ ਪ੍ਰਭੂ ਸੁਹਾਯੋ ॥੩੪॥

"Go inside and announce me, and offer him my salutation with joined hands." When the doorkeeper heard this, he made him wait there, and went in himself to where Prabhu[^10] sat in his splendour. (34)

ਰੁਖ਼ ਕੋ ਦੇਖਿ ਜੋਰਿ ਕਰ ਬੋਲਾ। 'ਸ਼੍ਰੀ ਪ੍ਰਭੁ ਨਿਜ ਦਾਸਨਿ ਕੋ ਓਲਾ!\
ਭੀਮ ਚੰਦ ਸੈਲਿੰਦ੍ਰ ਬਿਲੰਦੰ। ਪਠਯੋ ਵਕੀਲ ਨਮਹਿਂ ਕਰ ਬੰਦੰ ॥੩੫॥

He saw the Guru's mood, joined his hands and spoke. "Sri Prabhu, shelter of your servants! Bhim Chand, the lofty lord of the hills, has sent an envoy, who bows with joined hands. (35)

ਕਦਮ ਪਦਮ ਕੋ ਦਰਸ਼ਨ ਚਾਹਤਿ। ਕੁਛ ਕਾਰਜ ਹਿਤ ਲਖਿਯ ਉਮਾਹਤਿ।\
ਥਿਰਯੋ ਪੌਰ ਪਰ ਯਥਾ ਹੁਕਮ ਹ੍ਵੈ। ਕਰਹਿਂ ਸੁਨਾਵਨ, ਕ੍ਰਿਤੀ ਤਥਾ ਹ੍ਵੈ' ॥੩੬॥

"He longs for a sight of your lotus feet, and seems to be eager about some business. He is waiting at the gate. As your command is, so it will be done: shall we let him be heard?" (36)

ਸ਼੍ਰੀ ਕਲਗ਼ੀਧਰ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਸਾਚੇ। ਸੁਨਤਿ ਉਚਾਰੀ ਆਇਸੁ ਤਾਚੇ।\
'ਕਰਹਿਂ ਹਕਾਰਨਿ ਤਿਸ ਕੋ ਕਾਲੀ। ਦੇਗ਼ ਰਸਤ ਤੇ ਰਖਹੁ ਨ ਖਾਲੀ' ॥੩੭॥

Sri Kalgidhar,[^11] the true Satguru, heard him and gave his command. "We shall call him tomorrow. Do not leave him short of provisions from the degh."[^12] (37)

ਦ੍ਵਾਰਪਾਲ ਤਬਿ ਆਨਿ ਸੁਨਾਈ। ਆਛੀ ਮਾਨਿ ਗਯੋ ਨਿਜ ਥਾਂਈ।\
ਕਹਿ ਪੁਨ ਰਸਤ ਪੁਚਾਵਨਿ ਕਰੀ। ਖਾਨ ਪਾਨ ਕੀਨਸਿ ਬਿਧਿ ਖਰੀ ॥੩੮॥

The doorkeeper came and told him, and the envoy took it well and went back to his own quarters. Then word was given and the provisions were sent to him, and he ate and drank in good style. (38)

ਥਿਤ ਡੇਰੇ ਹੁਇ ਨਿਸਾ ਬਿਤਾਈ। ਜਾਗੇ ਪੁਨ ਪ੍ਰਭਾਤਿ ਹ੍ਵੈ ਆਈ।\
ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਕਰਿ ਸ਼ਨਾਨ ਸ਼ੁਭ ਨੀਰੰ। ਬੈਠਿ ਸਮਾਧਿ ਲਗਾਇ ਗਹੀਰੰ ॥੩੯॥

He stayed at his camp and passed the night. Dawn came, and all were awake again. The Satguru bathed in pure water, and sat in deep meditation. (39)

ਚਾਰੁ ਸ਼ਰੀਰ ਚੀਰ ਬਿਧਿ ਨੀਕੰ। ਬਾਂਧੀ ਜਿਗਾ ਰੁਚਿਰ ਉਸ਼ਨੀਕੰ।\
ਉਦਯੋ ਦਯੋਸਪਤਿ ਬਾਹਰਿ ਆਏ। ਕਲਗ਼ੀ ਸੁੰਦਰ ਸ਼ੋਭ ਵਧਾਏ ॥੪੦॥

He put fine clothes on his beautiful body, and tied a handsome jewelled ornament[^13] to his turban. As the sun rose he came out, and his beautiful kalgi added to his splendour. (40)

ਨੰਦ ਚੰਦ ਜੁਤਿ ਬ੍ਰਿੰਦ ਮਸੰਦੰ। ਪਦ ਅਰਬਿੰਦ ਬੰਦਿ ਕਰ ਬੰਦੰ।\
ਮਾਤੁਲ ਤਬਿ ਕ੍ਰਿਪਾਲ ਚਲਿ ਆਯੋ। ਭਗਨੀ ਨੰਦ ਬੰਦਿ ਸੁਖ ਪਾਯੋ ॥੪੧॥

Nand Chand[^14] came with the masaṅds[^15] and bowed with joined hands at his lotus feet. Then his mother's brother Kirpal[^16] came, bowed to his sister's son, and was glad. (41)

ਪਾਂਚਹੁ ਬੀਰ ਭਾਣਜੇ ਆਏ। ਬਸਤ੍ਰ ਸ਼ਸਤ੍ਰ ਤੇ ਅਧਿਕ ਸੁਹਾਏ।\
ਦਰਸ਼ਨ ਕਾਂਖੀ ਸੰਗਤਿ ਜੇਤੀ। ਲਏ ਉਪਾਇਨ ਪਹੁੰਚੀ ਤੇਤੀ ॥੪੨॥

The five warrior nephews[^17] came, splendid in their clothes and their arms. As many of the saṅgat as longed for a sight of him arrived, bringing their offerings. (42)

ਹੋਨਿ ਲਗੀ ਅਰਦਾਸ ਅਗਾਰੀ। ਹੇਰਿ ਹੇਰਿ ਹਰਖਤਿ ਹੁਇਂ ਵਾਰੀ।\
ਸਿੰਘਾਸਨ ਥਿਤ ਢੁਰਤੋ ਚੌਰ। ਬੈਠੇ ਕੁਲ ਸੋਢੀ ਸਿਰਮੌਰ ॥੪੩॥

The ardās[^18] began to be made before him; they looked and looked at him, rejoiced, and gave themselves over to him. He sat on the throne with the chaur[^19] waving over him, the crown of the Sodhi lineage.[^20] (43)

ਦਾਸਨ ਕਰੈਂ ਭਾਵਨਾ ਪੂਰੀ। ਸਭਿ ਤੇ ਭਈ ਸਭਾ ਸ਼ੁਭ ਪੂਰੀ।\
ਕਰੁਨਾ ਭਰੇ ਬਿਲੋਚਨ ਕੋਰਹਿਂ। ਹੇਰਹਿਂ ਪ੍ਰਿਯ ਸਿੱਖਨ ਕੀ ਓਰਹਿਂ ॥੪੪॥

He fulfilled the wishes of his servants, and the whole assembly was full. From the corners of his eyes, brimming with compassion, he looked toward his beloved Sikhs. (44)

---
*Thus in the Sri Gur Pratap Suraj Granth, in the first Rut, the thirty-first ansu, named "The Description of the Episode of the Coming of the Messenger".*

### Notes

[^1]: Prasadi is the elephant presented to the Guru by Raja Ratan Rai in Rut 1.21 to 1.23, the chapters that come just before the making of the war drum.
[^2]: The book prints a line saying that people hang on a warrior's arms. Bhai Vir Singh takes this for a copyist's slip of hoi for doi, "both", because the poet goes straight on to name the two worlds, this earth and the world of the gods, and because the same thought comes up again further on in the poem. The translation follows his reading.
[^3]: Vairāg is detachment: the turning of the heart away from the world and its pulls.
[^4]: Bhim Chand is the raja of Kahlur, whose capital was Bilaspur, the hill state nearest Anandpur. His visit to the Guru at Anandpur, where he saw the elephant, is told in the chapters just before this one, from Rut 1.28.
[^5]: The poet plays on one word used twice. Tīkā is the heir to a raja's seat, and tīkā is also the mark sent to a boy's house to seal a betrothal. The verse does not name the son; he is Ajmer Chand, who succeeded his father.
[^6]: Fateh Shah is the raja of Srinagar in Garhwal, in the hills to the east beyond the Jamuna. This is not the Srinagar of Kashmir.
[^7]: The poet's word is lāgī: the hereditary servants of a family, the barber among them, who carry its marriage business between the two houses.
[^8]: The poet's word is vakil, a ruler's agent or envoy sent to another court. In modern Punjabi it means a lawyer, which is not the sense here.
[^9]: The lord of elephants translates the poet's gajindra. Bhai Vir Singh reads it as the lion, or else as Airavat, the elephant of Indra, king of the gods.
[^10]: Prabhu, the Lord, is one of the names of the divine. The poet and the doorkeeper both use it here of the Guru himself.
[^11]: Kalgidhar, the wearer of the kalgi, is a name of Guru Gobind Singh Ji. The kalgi is the jewelled plume worn in his turban.
[^12]: The degh is the great cooking pot of the Guru's kitchen, from which everyone who came was fed. The Guru is ordering that the envoy be supplied from it for as long as he waits.
[^13]: The poet's word is jigha, a jewelled ornament fastened to the front of a turban.
[^14]: Nand Chand is the Guru's minister, who had the war drum made in Rut 1.24 and 1.25.
[^15]: The masaṅds were the agents the Guru appointed to lead the local saṅgats, gather their offerings and bring them in. Their conduct is the subject of much of Rut 3.
[^16]: Kirpal is Kirpal Chand, Mata Gujri Ji's brother and so the Guru's maternal uncle.
[^17]: The five nephews are the sons of Bibi Viro, the daughter of Guru Hargobind Sahib Ji, and so strictly the Guru's cousins; the poet counts them among the nephews of the house. Sango Shah, who spoke for sounding the drum in Rut 1.25, is one of them.
[^18]: The ardās is the standing prayer of petition, offered before the Guru.
[^19]: The chaur is the whisk waved over what is honoured, as it is waved today over Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji.
[^20]: The Sodhis are the family of the Gurus from Guru Ram Das Ji onward.
