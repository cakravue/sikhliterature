# Panth Prakash, Episode 13

## The sakhi of the tenth Patshah is written
### (sikh ubāran dushat saṅghāran: to save the Sikhs and destroy the wicked)

**[1-2]** After that the enmity of the Turks toward the
tenth Patshah[^1] grew. The hill chiefs, the rajas of the Shivalik states around Anandpur, carried
tales against him, and a spark went out from it and spread into a blaze. The qazis (judges of Islamic
law) and the Mughals schooled the Turks and made them forget the undertakings Babur had given.[^2]
*When did the Hindus ever give Hind away? Our own prophets gave it to us.* So they made the thing up out
of their own mouths.

**[3]** Guru Gobind Singh heard it, and anger rose in him, and he made the Panth to destroy the
Turks. He gave all four of his sons to be killed, and threw away his own line for the Panth.

**[4-5]** Then Murray[^3] asked me why the Guru had not kept the two of them together, his line and
his house. Where a man's own line goes on, I answered him, his love does not grow so much toward the
other. Dattatreya and Gorakh the Siddh (ancient sages) kept a panth and kept no seed of their own.
Muhammad too let his line go, and it was the higher house that he kept and made greater.

**[6-7]** Murray said, why then do you lay the blame on the Turks, when the Satguru worked it so by
his own understanding? The answer I gave him was this: they took the blood of the Sahibzade upon
their own heads. The Guru meant to grind the Turks down, and it was for that reason that the blame
came to rest on them. It was for that reason that he framed the Panth: to save the Sikhs and destroy
the wicked.

**[8-9]** The Guru laid his own body too to the Turks' account, wanting his Panth to grow. It is
either the panth or the line; even Ram and Krishan could not do both. Sri Ram and Krishan kept their
own sons and gave them the kingdom for love of them. Sri Satguru gave to his Sikhs, and by that a
greater way came about.

**[10]** While a man's own line is close beside him, his love for another's does not grow. Where
grass, the earth's own child, is coming up, no grain comes up in that field.

---
*Thus in the Sri Gur Panth Prakash, the thirteenth sakhi, the telling of the tenth Patshah.*

*Sri Gur Panth Prakash, Rattan Singh Bhangu, 1841. Gurmukhi text of record from the edition of the
Institute of Sikh Studies, Chandigarh, volume 1, episodes 1 to 81, with the English translation of
Kulwant Singh. Punjabi commentary from the Prachin Panth Prakash Steek, part 1, of Singh Sahib Baba
Santa Singh Akali, Jathedar of the 96 Krori Budha Dal. Both commentators are named in full in the
notes; the project docs abbreviate them KS and BSS. Cite as Panth Prakash 13.4.*

### Notes

[^1]: Patshah is the holder of supreme rule, a word the text uses of the Gurus and of the Mughals alike. The tenth Patshah is Guru Gobind Singh.
[^2]: The undertakings are those of episode 9 of this granth, where Guru Nanak tells Babur that the Lodhis will fall for their oppression and the Mughals rise in their place, and warns him that Mughal rule will last only so long as it is not itself oppressive.
[^3]: Captain David Murray, the English officer at Ludhiana at whose asking Bhangu told this whole granth; episode 2 tells how that came about. The poet writes the name both as Mari and as Mali.
