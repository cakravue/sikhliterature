# Panth Prakash, Episode 15

## The sakhi of the coming into being of the Khalsa Panth at Sri Kesgarh
### (aṅmrit saṅskār, pañj bhujaṅgī jo bhae: the Amrit ceremony, and the five bhujangis who were made)

**[1-2]** Having thought so much over, the Satguru had taken hold of the thing that was to be done,
and he sat at Sri Kesgarh[^1] and set about it. He called for pandits and great astrologers and had a
good hour fixed for it, and picked out five bhujangis,[^2] counting off five castes.

**[3-5]** He raised the five bhujangis up and had the four varnas (the four classes of Brahminical
law) filled into one. The first was Daya Singh, a Sobti (a Khatri sub-caste) by caste, and well known
as a man of Lahore. The second was Dharam Singh, who lived at Hastinapur, the ancient city on the
Ganges, a Jatt, brought to light from Shiva's matted hair. The third was Mohkam Singh, born at
Dwarka, Krishna's city on the coast of Gujarat, and he came as Namdev[^3] come again. The fourth was
Sahib Singh, a barber, from the place where Sain the bhagat had lived; the fifth is called Himmat
Singh, a water-carrier. So five bhujangis were taken, of five castes.

**[6-7]** He had the pāhul of the khanda[^4] made ready: water was set down, and sweets were brought
and put into it. He kept a dagger standing in it, and held the dagger with its edge turned toward
himself. Sri Satguru said Satnam and uttered Karta Purakh, and reciting Akal, Akal he turned the
sword through it in the proper way.

**[8-9]** First he read the pauri of the Var of Bhagauti,[^5] which he had put together himself at
that moment, and called the nine Patshahis to mind: *now be a help to the tenth.* Then he uttered the
thirty two svaiyas, which he had made with his own mouth; and the bani of Chandi, which is fierceness
upon fierceness, he set into the middle of the pāhul.

> *Tribhaṅgī Chhaṅd, Sri Dasam Granth.*
>
> ਖਗ ਖੰਡ ਬਿਹੰਡੰ ਖਲ ਦਲ ਖੰਡੰ, ਅਤਿ ਰਣ ਮੰਡੰ ਬਰ ਬੰਡੰ ॥\
> *khag khaṅd bihaṅdaṅ khal dal khaṅdaṅ, at raṇ maṅdaṅ bar baṅdaṅ*\
> Sword that shatters, that breaks the ranks of the wicked, that sets the field of battle raging and scatters the strong.
>
> ਭੁਜਦੰਡ ਅਖੰਡੰ ਤੇਜ ਪ੍ਰਚੰਡੰ, ਜੋਤਿ ਅਮੰਡੰ ਭਾਨ ਪ੍ਰਭੰ ॥\
> *bhujdaṅd akhaṅdaṅ tej prachaṅdaṅ, jot amaṅdaṅ bhān prabhaṅ*\
> Arm unbroken, fierceness at its full height, light that no one kindled, bright as the sun.
>
> ਸੁਖ ਸੰਤਾ ਕਰਣੰ ਦੁਰਮਤਿ ਦਰਣੰ, ਕਿਲਵਿਖ ਹਰਣੰ ਅਸਿ ਸਰਣੰ ॥\
> *sukh saṅtā karaṇaṅ durmat daraṇaṅ, kilvikh haraṇaṅ as saraṇaṅ*\
> Giver of ease to the saints, grinder of evil minds, taker away of sins: sword, I am in your refuge.
>
> ਜੈ ਜੈ ਜਗ ਕਾਰਣ ਸ੍ਰਿਸਿ ਉਬਾਰਣ, ਮਮ ਪ੍ਰਤਿਪਾਰਣ ਜੈ ਤੇਗੰ ॥\
> *jai jai jag kāraṇ srisi ubāraṇ, mam pratipāraṇ jai tegaṅ*\
> Hail, hail, cause of the world, deliverer of creation, my sustainer: hail to the sword.

**[10-12]** Sri Satguru filled his cupped hands the first time and called them forward. He had them
face him with their eyes held open, and cast it into them. He had them repeat Akal, Akal,
and had the Guru's fateh of Sri Waheguru called out. Then he poured five handfuls on each head, and
gave each of the five five draughts to drink. He mixed them all together, eating from one bowl, and
let no difference and no reserve be kept among them. The four varnas and the four ashrams (its four
stages of life): he had the janeū (the sacred thread of the twice-born) and the tilak (the mark on
the forehead) taken off.

**[13-14]** Then the Satguru gave them instruction. Have no dealings with Minas or masands,[^6] with
those who kill their daughters, with Ram Raiyas or with those who smoke; whoever joins with them will
come to ruin. Put into the Guru's offering box according to your means, and have karah prashad made
and share it with the Khalsa. Wear the kachhera (the breeches of the Sikh form) and bind a turban on
your head, and hold to the word of the Guru Granth.

**[15-17]** Saying this, Sri Satguru hung a sword at their necks and set dagger and quoit upon their
heads, with Akal spoken from the mouth. And he had them read Gurbani: Jap and Jaap to be recited at
both times, and Anand and Rehras, and the bani of Chandi to be read standing. Rise at both times and
bind the turban; keep your weapons about you all eight pahars (the whole of the day and the night).
Drink the amrit and go out hunting, so that the art of arms is kept up.

**[18-19]** Eat goat that has been killed with a single stroke (jhatka), and go nowhere near carrion or meat
killed in the Muslim way. Cherish the hair of your head, and do not cut a hair of it with a razor.
Let the rites for the ancestors go, and keep your attention at the Guru's feet. Whoever falls into
crooked ways, lay a tankhāh (the penance the Panth lays on an offender) on him without fail.

**[20]** The way the Satguru had done it the first time, that way he had it done again: from the five
bhujangis whom he had himself made, the Guru took pāhul.

**[21-22]** The bhujangis carried out the very same rite, and so Guru Gobind Singh was called Guru
and disciple both. This has been the way from the beginning, as Nanak took Angad as his Guru. He
drank the pāhul and put the Guru's word past question, and the Khalsa became a great holder of power.
In the year Sammat 1752 Bikrami (1695 CE), on a Wednesday in the month of Baisakh (April to May), the
Panth came into being.[^7]

---
*Thus in the Sri Gur Panth Prakash, the fifteenth sakhi, the telling of how the Khalsa Panth came
into being at Sri Kesgarh.*

*Sri Gur Panth Prakash, Rattan Singh Bhangu, 1841. Gurmukhi text of record from the edition of the
Institute of Sikh Studies, Chandigarh, volume 1, episodes 1 to 81, with the English translation of
Kulwant Singh. Punjabi commentary from the Prachin Panth Prakash Steek, part 1, of Singh Sahib Baba
Santa Singh Akali, Jathedar of the 96 Krori Budha Dal. Both commentators are named in full in the
notes; the project docs abbreviate them KS and BSS. Cite as Panth Prakash 15.6.*

### Notes

[^1]: The fort at Anandpur where the Khalsa was made, now one of the five Takhts. In Baba Santa Singh Akali's account it was called Divan Garh before this, a name Guru Tegh Bahadur gave it because the diwan was held there and the saṅgats came to it for darshan and rested there.
[^2]: bhujaṅgī: literally a young snake, and so a young Singh, sharp from birth. It is Bhangu's standing word for the Guru's picked men through this episode and the next, and "young Singh" loses the image the word is carrying. · Baba Santa Singh Akali sets aside the familiar tradition that the Guru had goats tied in the tent behind him and cut them instead of the five men. He calls it a joke made by people who never took the Khalsa's form themselves, and holds that what the Guru did there came out of his own power and needs no such explaining.
[^3]: The fourteenth-century bhagat whose hymns are in the Guru Granth Sahib, a calico-printer by birth, which is Mohkam Singh's own trade. Sain, below, is likewise a bhagat of the Granth, and a barber, as Sahib Singh is.
[^4]: pāhul: the draught by which a Sikh is initiated; khanda: the double-edged sword it is prepared with. In Baba Santa Singh Akali's account the sweets were patase, and it was Mata Sahib Devan who brought them and poured them into the bowl.
[^5]: The Var of Bhagauti is Chandi di Var, and its first pauri, which calls on the Gurus in order, is the one recited at the opening of every ardās to this day. Baba Santa Singh Akali holds that the Guru composed that pauri on the spot, at this moment, and that the maryada of beginning with ardās was fixed here.
[^6]: Minas are the followers of Prithi Chand, the eldest son of Guru Ram Das, who set up a rival line. Masands were the agents appointed by the Gurus to lead the local saṅgats and bring in their offerings; by this time the office had gone bad, and Baba Santa Singh Akali says plainly that they were collecting the daswandh and eating it at home. Ram Raiyas are the followers of Ram Rai, the elder son of Guru Har Rai, who was put out of the Panth for altering a line of Gurbani in Aurangzeb's court.
[^7]: The Khalsa was in fact made on the Vaisakhi of Sammat 1756 Bikrami, 1699 CE, at Anandpur. The year in the verse is four short of it, and Kulwant Singh says so in his own notes.
